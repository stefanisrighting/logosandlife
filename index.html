Classical Arguments

Kalam Cosmological Argument
Classical · Origin of the Universe

Everything that begins to exist has a cause. The universe began to exist. Therefore, the universe has a cause — one that is timeless, spaceless, immaterial, and enormously powerful.

P1: Whatever begins to exist has a cause.
P2: The universe began to exist. (Big Bang, BGV Theorem)
∴ The universe has a cause beyond itself.

Key verse: Genesis 1:1 — "In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth."

Fine-Tuning Argument
Classical · Teleological

The fundamental constants of physics are calibrated to astonishing precision for life to exist. The odds of this arising by chance are astronomically small — design is the most reasonable inference.

P1: The fine-tuning of the universe is due to physical necessity, chance, or design.
P2: It is not due to necessity or chance.
∴ Therefore it is due to design. (Robin Collins)

Key verse: Psalm 19:1 — "The heavens declare the glory of God."

Moral Argument
Classical · Ethics

If objective moral truths exist — things that are truly right or wrong regardless of opinion — then a moral law-giver must exist. Most people accept moral realism in practice, even if they deny it in theory.

P1: If God does not exist, objective moral values do not exist.
P2: Objective moral values do exist. (C.S. Lewis, William Lane Craig)
∴ Therefore God exists.

Key verse: Romans 2:15 — "…the law written on their hearts."

Evidential Arguments

Minimal Facts: Resurrection
Evidential · Historical

Using only facts accepted by the vast majority of critical historians — including skeptics — the resurrection of Jesus is the best explanation for the data: empty tomb, post-mortem appearances, origin of the disciples' belief.

F1: Jesus died by crucifixion.
F2: The tomb was found empty.
F3: Many reported seeing Jesus risen.
Best explanation: bodily resurrection. (Gary Habermas)

Key verse: 1 Corinthians 15:3–8 — eyewitness creed dated within years of the event.

Archaeological Reliability
Evidential · Archaeology

Over and over, archaeology has confirmed biblical details dismissed as legendary — the Pool of Siloam, Pontius Pilate inscription, the city of Jericho, the Hittites, King David's existence. The Bible is historically trustworthy.

Key resource: "The Bible Unearthed" for contrast; "Is the Bible True?" by Jeffery Sheler for affirmation.

Presuppositional Arguments

Transcendental Argument
Presuppositional · Logic

Logic, mathematics, and the uniformity of nature are preconditions of intelligibility. These things only make sense if a rational, consistent God upholds the universe. The unbeliever must borrow from the Christian worldview to argue against it.

P1: Knowledge requires laws of logic, induction, and morality.
P2: These are only grounded in the biblical God.
∴ The biblical God is the precondition of knowledge. (Van Til, Bahnsen)

Key verse: Colossians 2:3 — "in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge."
